I am going to say the unkind thing, because it is sitting on the table.

Americans are grumping about fuel in machines the size of a small room. Europeans are looking at the same week and feeling something closer to fear. That difference is not an accident of personality.

Part of it is arithmetic. This month European wholesale gas has been trading near twenty-eight dollars per million BTU. The American benchmark has been under three. Ten to one, for the same molecule, on the same planet, in the same week.

That gap did not fall out of the sky. It was built. Europe shut in its own production and leaned on imported gas, then lost the cheap piped version of it and replaced it with cargoes that arrive at whatever the world will pay that morning. It priced carbon into the bill deliberately, which is what pricing carbon is for. Whatever you think of the reasons — and some of them are good reasons — the effect is a continent that buys its heat at auction. We sit on ours.

At the meter the gap is smaller than the wholesale number makes it sound. A Dutch household was paying about one euro fifty-six a cubic metre this month. An American pays nearer sixty cents. Not ten to one. But three to one is still the difference between a bill and a decision.

We fill a Super SUV and complain. We should. Money is money. But we still have a continent of oil, a loud argument, and a cupboard that mostly fills. The flinch at our pump is a mood. In a lot of Europe the flinch is the week: the loaf, the radiator, the fourth-floor room that already cooked people this summer while the speeches called it transition.

## What the summer actually cost

They just came off a heat that sat on the old and the alone. Homes not built for those nights. Policies that made power precious and then acted surprised when the body kept score.

France counted. Three heat waves in one summer, and a number for each.

At least 5,764 excess deaths between 17 June and 2 July — the worst any French heat wave has produced since 2003. At least 1,243 more across the July wave, in the hottest month France has recorded since it started keeping the record in 1900. At least 817 more between 27 July and 20 August, in a heat that tied the longest ever measured there. That third number was published yesterday. The consolidated figure comes in the autumn, and it will be bigger than what we have now.

Add what is on the table: close to eight thousand people. Provisional. Official. Not a metaphor.

On 24 and 25 June, France averaged thirty degrees across a full twenty-four hours for the first time since anyone has been writing it down. Bordeaux reached 42.5. Paris reached 40.1. More than half the June deaths landed inside three days.

You do not come off a summer like that and then take a shipping scare as entertainment.

If the lanes stay ugly — war-risk premiums up, hulls routed the long way around trouble — and China goes from no camera to a calendar that breaks, they do not receive that as a seminar. They receive it as dinner. We receive it as a more expensive Saturday and a segment on the evening news.

## Two kitchens

See the split. One public is invited to treat energy as a lifestyle complaint. The other is invited to treat it as weather it cannot vote off the window. Keep the first group annoyed but mobile. Keep the second group cold, hot, or dear, and grateful for whatever cap the capital announces. That is a very old way to run a neighborhood. Call it climate, call it union, call it strategy. The plate knows.

I should be fair about my own side. Something like a quarter of American households said they struggled with an energy bill last year. Europe's own measure — people who cannot keep the house warm enough — runs under nine percent. Those two numbers are not counting the same thing and I am not going to pretend they are. The point was never that Americans are comfortable. Plenty of us are not. The point is what the bill is attached to. Ours is attached to a market we are allowed to shout at. Theirs is attached to a policy they were told was weather.

I will be fine in the ordinary American way. That is not a virtue. It is geography and a market that still knows how to drill and argue. What I cannot do is pretend their fear is the same animal as our grumble. It is not. And if you wanted a public that would accept the next round of "temporary" measures, you could hardly design a better year than the one they just had — heat, bills, and a map that will not sit still.

Scotland and Wales can want out of London. I get that. Joining the room that just lived this summer is a separate decision, and it should be made with the lights on.

El Salvador is the ugly little lesson, and it cuts both ways. Bukele broke the gangs, and the country is growing again — the Fund expects about four and a half percent this year, which is not nothing for a place people were leaving on foot. He did it by hollowing out the courts and running an emergency that never ends. And he still finished up inside a one-point-four-billion-dollar IMF programme that tells him what he may and may not do with his own reserves. You can stand up small and try to eat. You will still be eating on somebody's terms. The only question is whose kitchen.

I am not rewriting the China page. That page is the vacant chair. This page is the two kitchens. Ours is loud. Theirs is watching the news like it might come through the door.

— Caroline North
