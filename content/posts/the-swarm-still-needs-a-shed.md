I do not sleep better because a general used the word “swarm.” I also refuse to pretend the sky over Charlotte is already full of someone else’s air force.

The picture that travels is simple: a thousand cheap birds, no warning, every power plant and ballpark on the same night. The picture leaves out the boring part. Someone has to build those birds, pack them, move them, talk to them, and do it again tomorrow. A swarm that matters is not a spell. It is a warehouse, a truck, a ship, a barge, a cargo bay, a radio, a crew.

Ukraine proved the ugly version. They put drones in containers and drove them to the target. That is not magic. That is logistics with a lid on. Russia’s cheap-drone war runs on factories and stockpiles. Even our own Air Force is asking whether a cargo plane can be a mothership — which is a polite way of saying the swarm needs a ride.

So the nightmare that skips the shed is a movie. The real problem is smaller and nastier: a few dozen, maybe a few hundred, launched from something you can hide in plain sight. Parks, bases, and plants have already seen prowlers. That is worth a fence and a watchman. It is not the same sentence as “the continent goes dark at dusk.”

In September the Pentagon said it would spend a year flying lasers and microwaves on actual bases — power, cooling, whether the thing still works when the kitchen is open. That is a tool against cheap electronics in the air. It is not an EMP that folds a city. People use one word for both. The bills are not cousins.

Could a determined enemy hurt a refinery, a stadium, a substation? Yes. Could they do it without a footprint we are allowed to look for? That is the part the siren skips. Find the container. Find the ship. Jam the link. Make the second wave expensive. Those are grown-up jobs. They are not as photogenic as a pulse over a skyline.

I remain a skeptic of the grand blackout. I am not a skeptic of a truck. If your fear needs a bald man and a cat, you will miss the shed. If your calm needs a promise that nothing flies, you will miss the truck too.

The birds are cheap. The circus that launches them is not. That is not comfort. It is a place to put your eyes.

— Julian B. Horrow
