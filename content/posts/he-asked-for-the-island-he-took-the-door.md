In mid-January, DJT put a price on eight European countries for standing with Denmark over Greenland. Ten percent in February. Twenty-five percent in June if there was no “complete and total purchase.” Troops had gone up there to show the flag. He answered with a tariff list: Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, Britain, the Netherlands, Finland.

That was the loud part. People heard “make it a state.”

On 21 January, in Davos, he stepped off the tariff. He ruled out force. He said the United States and NATO had a “framework” for Greenland and the whole Arctic — security, minerals, “a deal that’s forever.” Mark Rutte said the question of who owns the island did not even come up that night.

So the file is not a purchase. Denmark still holds the paper. Greenland is not a star on the flag. What moved was access, exercises, a veto on who invests, and the temperature of the tariff threat.

Why put this in a house that just opened in September? Because the trick repeats. He names the maximum. The room panics about the name. The signature, if there is one, is smaller and harder: bases, routes, who gets a say when China writes a check. Ireland in September is the same family of weather. Cardiff is the decoy headline. Greenland was the first time this year he showed the pattern in public and then took the off-ramp.

17 January: the tariff list.

21 January: the framework, the threat pulled.

Sovereignty: unchanged.

What he wanted in the speech: the island.

What sat on the table when the talking stopped: the door to it.

If you only remember the statehood line, you remember the billboard. The clerk keeps the framework.

— Sunday Editor
