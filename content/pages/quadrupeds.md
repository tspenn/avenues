# Marine 4Ped

*A work machine for places people and wheels cannot go.*

*Skyland Reach LLC*

Coming soon. The machine is defined. How it is sealed, cooled, and serviced is not on this page.

## The Problem

Some of the most valuable work in the world happens where a person should not be and a wheeled machine cannot go: the sea floor around an oil rig, a plant deck in a chemical fire, a desert far from any road.

What gets sent there today is usually a diver, a crane and a crew on a vessel that costs a fortune a day, or a robot built for a clean factory floor and wrapped in protection as an afterthought. The wrapping wears through. The vessel days remain.

## The Machine

We are building a compact, four-limbed work robot from the environment inward rather than from the factory outward. It scrambles on all fours across structure the way an animal does, and it braces itself to work with real force.

The lead product is **Marine 4Ped** — subsea inspection, maintenance and repair. Vessel days are the number that industry already measures and already wants gone. A machine that can live at the worksite and do the work removes those days.

**Arid 4Ped** is the same architecture on land: abrasive, remote, or disaster ground where wheels fail.

The ape-like form is not the invention. That form already exists. What makes this machine worth building is a specific engineering approach we are not describing here.

## Who This Is For

Operators and service companies in offshore energy. Program offices funding work in hazardous or remote environments. Investors who already understand vessel-day economics.
