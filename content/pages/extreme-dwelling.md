## The Problem

The hardest places on earth to build are the coldest and the driest. Arctic ground swallows roads and foundations. Desert defeats water, heat, and sand. They are also, increasingly, where work, resources, and strategy are moving.

Ordinary construction fights those environments and loses. The cost of keeping a conventional camp alive there is the story.

## The Approach

We are developing a settlement system for those two extremes — frozen and arid — that captures what the site already gives. One architecture, two climates.

The enclosure is meant to turn incoming light into interior light, heat, and power. The ground beneath is treated as a foundation and a source of water rather than as a liability. That water supports food production inside. A hybrid energy design is there so the enclave is not a fair-weather idea: it has to hold through polar night or a desert summer.

Individual pieces draw on methods already proven in cold-climate agriculture, large glazing, and cold-ground engineering. The novelty is the integration. That integration is not described here.
